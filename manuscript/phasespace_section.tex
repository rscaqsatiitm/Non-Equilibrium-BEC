\section{Phase-space kinetic model}
\label{sec:phasespace}
To remove the ergodic assumption altogether we have solved the ZNG kinetic
equation for the thermal cloud in phase space with the test-particle method of
Jackson and Zaremba~\cite{Jackson2002}, in a model of the actual trap of the
experiment. The potential is the sum of two Gaussian beams of waists $35$ and
$45\,\mu$m crossing at $40^\circ$ in the horizontal plane, gravity, and the
force of the quadrupole field of the K MOT, which is on during the last $6$~s
of the evaporation and acts on the $|F=1,m_F=-1\rangle$ atoms with
$\mu_B B'/2$. Here, the beam depths are scaled together so that the
geometric-mean frequency at the displaced minimum follows the calibrated ramp
of Ref.~\cite{MelladoMunoz2020}, and the magnetic force is reduced to two
parameters, a vertical effective gravity $g_{\rm eff}$ and a horizontal
acceleration $f_x$ along the weak axis of the trap, because the position of
the quadrupole centre relative to the cloud is not known to us
[CONFIRM?]. These two parameters fix the effective trap depth, which we
compute as the lowest energy at which the basin of the minimum connects to
the outside (a watershed on a $96^3$ grid), and hence the evaporation:
an atom leaves when its trajectory crosses the saddle, with no truncation
energy imposed by hand. The thermal atoms are represented by $4\times10^4$
test particles moving in the trap and in the Hartree-Fock mean field $2gn_c$
of a Thomas-Fermi condensate in the local harmonic approximation.
Thermal-thermal collisions are sampled with the direct-simulation Monte Carlo
method in cells of $1.5\,\mu$m with the Bose enhancement $(1+f_3)(1+f_4)$,
where $f$ is the local Bose distribution fitted to the density and kinetic
energy of each cell, corrected for the measured excess of slow particles.
The exchange with the condensate is treated in a hybrid way: at every fifth
time step the energy distribution of the test particles is binned, the
ergodic $C_{12}$ integral of Sec.~\ref{sec:model} is evaluated on it, and the
resulting transfer is realised by removing or creating test particles on the
corresponding energy shells. This keeps the exchange rate identical to the
one validated against detailed balance in the ergodic model, while the
thermal cloud itself is free to be anisotropic and non-ergodic. We verified
that the thermal-thermal collision rate reproduces the classical value
$\bar n\sigma\bar v_{\rm rel}$ to $20\%$, that an anisotropic velocity
distribution relaxes in $2$ to $3$ collisions per atom, that the exchange
kernel obeys detailed balance in a homogeneous gas (out- to in-event ratio
$0.97\pm0.04$ at saturation and $1.81\pm0.08$ for a thermal chemical
potential $0.6\,k_BT$ below $\mu_c$, against $1$ and $e^{0.6}=1.82$), and
that a cloud $1.6$ times above saturation at $T=150$~nK grows a condensate
from a one-atom seed to $10^3$ atoms in $0.1$~s [SIM]. The observables are
extracted from synthetic time-of-flight images: the thermal test particles
are propagated ballistically for $20$~ms, the condensate is added as a
Thomas-Fermi profile expanded with the Castin-Dum scaling, and the image in
the vertical plane is fitted with a Gaussian plus a Thomas-Fermi column
density with every pixel weighted equally, as in the experiment. Applied to
a saturated Bose cloud without condensate this analysis returns a
"condensed fraction" of $0.17$, and with condensed fractions of $0.27$ and
$0.50$ it returns $0.34$ and $0.56$ with Gaussian temperatures $20$ to $30\%$
below the kinetic ones [SIM]: the bimodal fit assigns the Bose-enhanced peak
of a nearly saturated cloud partly to the condensate.

\subsection{Trap depth and the evaporation ramp}
The first result of the phase-space model concerns the trap itself. With the
nominal waists, the effective depth falls faster than the beam depth
$U\propto\bar\omega^2w^2$ because the tilt removes a growing fraction of it
as the power is lowered, and for every static pair $(g_{\rm eff},f_x)$ the
ratio of the depths at $\bar\omega/2\pi=136$ and $80$~Hz (the calibrated
frequencies at $t=1$ and $2$~s) exceeds $7$, whereas the ergodic calibration
of Sec.~\ref{sec:calib} requires $\eta=U/k_BT\simeq5$ at $t=1$~s and
$4.5$ at $t=2$~s, that is, a ratio of $4.1$ [SIM]. A horizontal force above
$0.1\,g$ makes things worse: the beams can no longer hold the atoms at
$\bar\omega/2\pi<105$~Hz, so the ramp cannot be completed. The closest
static compromise, $g_{\rm eff}=0.7\,g$ and $f_x=0.1\,g$, gives
$2.3\,\mu$K, $1.07\,\mu$K and $183$~nK at $136$, $108$ and $80$~Hz, against
$1.2\,\mu$K, $585$~nK and $290$~nK required [SIM]. With this trap the
evaporation is too slow until $t\simeq1.6$~s and too fast after it, and the
condensate appears at $t=1.85$~s instead of $1.15$~s. At $t=2$~s the model
gives $N=2.0\times10^4$, $T=46$~nK and $F=0.15$ without K
($2.0\times10^4$, $65$~nK, $0.64$ measured), $N=1.35\times10^4$ and $F=0.01$
with K ($1.26\times10^4$, $0.46$) and $N=6.4\times10^3$ and $F=0$ with
$4\times$K ($6.0\times10^3$, $0.23$) [SIM]: the atom numbers follow the
measured loss law, the temperatures follow the data to $20\%$ until
$t=1.8$~s, and the condensed fractions do not, because the model cloud
reaches saturation $0.7$~s later than the real one. We therefore do not
calibrate the phase-space model on the ramp; the real trap must have
been shallower at $t=1$~s than two Gaussian beams with a static tilt allow,
which points to a time-dependent magnetic force or to larger effective
waists [CONFIRM?]. Two kinetic results of the ramp runs survive this
caveat. First, once saturation is reached the condensate grows at
$1.0\times10^4$ atoms/s at $T\simeq120$~nK, limited by the rate at which
thermal-thermal collisions feed the low-energy states and not by the
exchange with the condensate: with the exchange switched off the occupation
of the states within $4\hbar\bar\omega$ of the band bottom rises to six
times the saturated Bose value in $0.3$~s, and with it switched on a
one-atom seed grows to $10^3$ atoms in $0.1$~s [SIM]. Second, the
evaporation through the saddle cools the vertical direction preferentially
but the thermal cloud stays nearly isotropic, $T_y/T_z\le1.2$ at $t=2$~s,
because the collision rate of $5$ to $15$~Hz keeps the axes within
$2$--$3$ collisions of each other [SIM]: the factor-of-two anisotropy of
the moment model of Sec.~\ref{sec:aniso} is an upper bound set by its
single-direction escape rule, not a property of the real trap.

\subsection{Hold phase and the relaxation time}
The hold phase does not depend on the ramp calibration, because it starts
from the measured state, and it is where the time scale of the relaxation
can be read off. We take the trap at the calibrated $\bar\omega/2\pi=99$~Hz
and the initial state from the first points of Fig.~S2(ii):
$N=1.94\times10^4$, $T=112$~nK, $F=0.20$ for the sample evaporated without
K, and $N=1.57\times10^4$, $T=111$~nK, $F=0.18$ for the sample evaporated
with K. Here, the thermal atoms are sampled from the Bose distribution with
the fugacity that reproduces the measured thermal number at the measured
temperature, $z=0.99$ and $0.87$ respectively, so that the sample evaporated
with K starts $13\%$ below saturation, as the measured $\mu_{\rm BEC}$ of
Fig.~2(d) indicates. The measured atom number falls from $1.94\times10^4$
to $1.05\times10^4$ during the first $2$~s of the hold, which a background
loss of $0.12$~s$^{-1}$ cannot produce; the trap must still evaporate, and
indeed the ergodic calibration of Sec.~\ref{sec:hold} found
$\eta=3.4$ for the hold. We therefore ran two cases: a deep trap
($g_{\rm eff}=0.7\,g$, $f_x=0.1\,g$, depth $700$~nK, $\eta=6.3$) in which
evaporation is negligible, and a shallow one ($g_{\rm eff}=g$,
$f_x=0.1\,g$, depth $362$~nK, $\eta=3.2$) that reproduces the measured
atom number ($1.02\times10^4$ and $8.3\times10^3$ at $2$~s against
$1.05\times10^4$ and $9.0\times10^3$ measured) [SIM].

In the deep trap the condensate of the sample evaporated with K is depleted
with a time constant of $175$~ms from $F=0.18$ to $0.07$, and that of the
sample without K with $170$~ms from $0.20$ to $0.15$ [SIM]: in contact with
a thermal cloud of the measured number and temperature, the condensate
does not survive for $3.9$~s, in a model with the real trap geometry, a
non-ergodic thermal cloud and an exchange rate validated against detailed
balance. This confirms the conclusion of the ergodic model of
Sec.~\ref{sec:hold} with a time constant three times longer, the difference
being the finite rate at which the test-particle cloud redistributes the
atoms it receives.

The shallow trap, shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:pshold}, changes the picture. The
condensate of the sample evaporated with K is first depleted, from $2870$ to
$2160$ atoms in $0.1$~s, and then grows again because the evaporation cools
the cloud: the model gives $F=0.18$, $0.25$, $0.34$, $0.40$ and $0.43$ at
$0.2$, $0.5$, $1$, $1.5$ and $2$~s, against $0.18$, $0.26$, $0.38$, $0.46$
and $0.48$ measured, and for the sample without K $0.26$, $0.36$, $0.45$,
$0.51$ and $0.55$ against $0.34$, $0.43$, $0.56$, $0.63$ and $0.66$ [SIM].
The atom numbers and condensed fractions of both samples are thus
reproduced to within $0.1$ in $F$ with no adjusted parameter beyond the
trap depth. The temperatures are not: the model cloud cools from $100$ to
$63$~nK within the first $0.2$~s and then to $49$~nK at $2$~s in both
samples, whereas the measured temperature falls from $112$ to $57$~nK for
the sample without K and only from $111$ to $81$~nK for the sample with K.
The synthetic images give the same temperature as the kinetic one
($51$ against $50$~nK at $2$~s), so the discrepancy is not an artefact of
the bimodal fit in the model. In other words, the number of atoms and the
condensed fraction of the dissipated sample during the hold are those of a
cloud in kinetic equilibrium at $50$ to $60$~nK, while its Gaussian
temperature is $80$ to $100$~nK. Section~\ref{sec:NE} shows that the
measured states themselves sit close to the equilibrium at their own number
and energy, with an equilibrium temperature $T'$ of $70$~nK at $2$~s for the
sample with K. The kinetic temperature of the test-particle cloud is not a
Bose temperature: a saturated Bose cloud has a kinetic temperature of
$0.9\,T$, and the test-particle cloud over-populates the low-energy states
by a further $10$ to $15\%$, so its $50$~nK correspond to a Bose temperature
of about $60$~nK, $15\%$ below $T'$ and $25\%$ below the measured value.
The phase-space model therefore reproduces $N$ and $F$ with a thermal cloud
that holds somewhat less energy than the measured state, the same model-side
deficit as in the ergodic model with K (Sec.~\ref{sec:disc}), and it does
not single out the measured temperature as the quantity at fault. What it
does establish is the time scale: with the real trap and a non-ergodic
cloud, every departure from the fixed-$(N,E)$ equilibrium of the size seen
in the data relaxes in $0.1$ to $0.2$~s, so the $3.9$~s of Fig.~4 cannot be
a collisional relaxation. The arms of the crossed trap are the one feature
of the real geometry that this model still lacks: an atom leaving the
crossing region along a beam stays radially bound and returns after half
an axial period of $2$ to $4$~s for a beam depth of $0.5\,\mu$K
[CONFIRM?: wavelength and beam powers], and such a population would raise
the horizontal Gaussian width without taking part in the exchange with the
condensate. Adding the axial beam profile to the trap model is
straightforward once the beam parameters are known.

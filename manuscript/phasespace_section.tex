\section{Phase-space kinetic model}
\label{sec:phasespace}
To remove the ergodic assumption altogether we have solved the ZNG kinetic
equation for the thermal cloud in phase space with the test-particle method of
Jackson and Zaremba~\cite{Jackson2002}, in the actual trap of the experiment.
The trap is the sum of two Gaussian beams of waists $35$ and $45\,\mu$m
crossing at $40^\circ$ in the horizontal plane, plus gravity. Here, the beam
depths are scaled together so that the geometric-mean frequency at the
(gravity-shifted) minimum follows the calibrated ramp of
Ref.~\cite{MelladoMunoz2020}; the only free quantity of the potential is the
effective gravity $g_{\rm eff}$, because the quadrupole field of the K MOT,
which is on during the last $6$~s of the evaporation, exerts a force of
$\mu_B B'/2$ on the $|F=1,m_F=-1\rangle$ atoms and partly levitates them
($B'=10$~G/cm cancels one third of gravity). This parameter decides the
effective trap depth: the saddle point below the minimum lies $2.1\,\mu$K
above it at $t=1$~s and $65$~nK at $t=2$~s for $g_{\rm eff}=g$, against
$3.7\,\mu$K and $440$~nK for $g_{\rm eff}=g/2$ [SIM]. Evaporation is
therefore not modelled by a truncation energy but happens dynamically: an
atom leaves when its trajectory crosses the saddle, which selects the
vertical direction and the vertical energy. The thermal atoms are
represented by $6\times10^4$ test particles (about one per atom) moving in
the trap and the Hartree-Fock mean field $2gn_c$ of a Thomas-Fermi
condensate in the local harmonic approximation; thermal-thermal collisions
are sampled with the direct-simulation Monte Carlo method in cells of
$1.5\,\mu$m with the Bose enhancement $(1+f_3)(1+f_4)$, and the exchange
with the condensate with the Monte Carlo rules of Ref.~\cite{Jackson2002}
[their Eqs.~(35) and (37)], where the occupation numbers $f$ are those of the
local Bose distribution fitted to the density and kinetic energy of each
cell and the "in" partner is the test particle of the cell closest in
velocity to the kinematically required one, with its energy accounted
exactly. We verified that the thermal-thermal collision rate reproduces the
classical value $\bar n\sigma\bar v_{\rm rel}$ to $20\%$, that an anisotropic
velocity distribution relaxes in $2$ to $3$ collisions per atom, and that
the exchange with the condensate obeys detailed balance: in a homogeneous
gas the ratio of out- to in-events is $0.97\pm0.04$ at saturation and
$1.81\pm0.08$ for a thermal chemical potential $0.6\,k_BT$ below $\mu_c$,
against $1$ and $e^{0.6}=1.82$ [SIM]. The observables are extracted from
synthetic time-of-flight images: the thermal test particles are propagated
ballistically for $20$~ms, the condensate is added as a Thomas-Fermi profile
expanded with the Castin-Dum scaling, and the image in the vertical plane is
fitted with a Gaussian plus a Thomas-Fermi column density with every pixel
weighted equally, as in the experiment. Applied to a saturated Bose cloud
without condensate this analysis returns a "condensed fraction" of $0.17$,
and with condensed fractions of $0.27$ and $0.50$ it returns $0.34$ and
$0.56$ with Gaussian temperatures $20$ to $30\%$ below the kinetic ones
[SIM]: the bimodal fit assigns the Bose-enhanced peak of a nearly saturated
cloud partly to the condensate.

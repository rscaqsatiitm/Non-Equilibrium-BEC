\title{Kinetic theory of dissipative distillation: finite reservoir and energy conservation}
\author{[AUTHORS?]}
\affiliation{[AFFILIATION?]}

\begin{abstract}
We solve the ergodic quantum-kinetic equations of a trapped Bose gas, with
Bose-enhanced collisions between the thermal atoms and between thermal atoms
and a Thomas-Fermi condensate, for the dissipative distillation experiment of
Mellado Mu\~noz \emph{et al.} [Phys. Rev. Lett. \textbf{125}, 020403 (2020)],
in which an evaporating $^{87}$Rb cloud loses atoms uniformly in energy to a
cold $^{39}$K gas. Unlike the growth equations used so far, the model keeps
the full energy distribution of a thermal cloud of $10^4$ atoms, conserves
number and energy, and lets the temperature follow from the dynamics. After a
single calibration of the evaporation ramp on the data without K, the model
reproduces the atom number and the condensed fraction of the distillation data
to better than $0.05$ in $F$, with and without K and at four times the K
density, and the condensed fraction during the hold after the K atoms are
removed. It does not reproduce the measured temperatures with K, which
exceed the kinetic ones by 30 to 45\%, and it predicts that the undersaturated
thermal clouds inferred from them refill from the condensate within 50~ms,
against the 3.9~s relaxation observed. The discrepancy exceeds the factor of
two to four by which the condensate-thermal collision integral is uncertain.
We discuss which measurement would separate a non-thermal momentum
distribution from a genuine suppression of the condensate-thermal exchange,
and show analytically that part of the excess fraction reported relative to
the constant-temperature equilibrium curve is removed when the comparison is
made at fixed energy.
\end{abstract}

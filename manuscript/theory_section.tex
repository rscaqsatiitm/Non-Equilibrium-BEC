\title{Kinetic theory of dissipative distillation: finite reservoir and energy conservation}
\author{[AUTHORS?]}
\affiliation{[AFFILIATION?]}

\begin{abstract}
We solve the ergodic quantum-kinetic equations of a trapped Bose gas, with
Bose-enhanced collisions between the thermal atoms and between thermal atoms
and a Thomas-Fermi condensate, for the dissipative distillation experiment of
Mellado Mu\~noz \emph{et al.} [Phys. Rev. Lett. \textbf{125}, 020403 (2020)],
in which an evaporating $^{87}$Rb cloud loses atoms uniformly in energy to a
cold $^{39}$K gas. Unlike the growth equations used so far, the model keeps
the full energy distribution of a thermal cloud of $10^4$ atoms, conserves
number and energy, and lets the temperature follow from the dynamics. After a
single calibration of the evaporation ramp on the data without K, the
ergodic model reproduces the atom number and the condensed fraction of the
distillation data to better than $0.05$ in $F$, with and without K and at
four times the K density, and a phase-space model with the real trap
geometry reproduces the hold after the K atoms are removed from the
measured initial states. Both relax any measured state to the equilibrium
at its own atom number and energy within $0.05$ to $0.2$~s. Computed from
the data, that equilibrium lies within $0.03$ of the measured condensed
fraction for the sample without K and within $0.10\pm0.03$ for the samples
with K, against about $0.29\pm0.1$ for the constant-temperature reference used
so far: two thirds of the reported excess is the reference, and the $3.9$~s
decay of the excess is the drift of number and energy by loss and residual
evaporation at fixed trap depth rather than a thermalization time. The
residual of the samples with K is constant in time, of the size of the
interaction shift in the equilibrium formula, and equivalent to a $10$ to
$20\%$ offset of the measured temperature; the models also arrive at the
measured number and fraction with $20$ to $25\%$ less energy than the data
when K is present. We state which refit of the existing images separates
these possibilities.
\end{abstract}
